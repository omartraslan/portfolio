The first 30 minutes

The first half hour of a major incident sets its direction. In that window I establish a clear shared understanding of the situation, define roles and accountability, and focus the team on mitigating customer impact before pursuing root cause. Communication starts at once, to internal and external stakeholders, on a fixed cadence, so everyone works from the same information while we manage against SLAs.

Alongside technical resolution, leadership needs consistent, timely information. The next section covers how I structure executive and customer communication during major incidents.

Executive and customer communication

During a major incident, executives need the same facts, from one source, at a predictable time. Each update states the customer impact, the customer communications plan, the recovery estimate and whether leadership needs to act. Updates go out on schedule even when there is no new information, because a missed update creates more inbound questions than an unchanged status does.

Update format

Customer impact

Which customers are affected and their tiers, what those customers and their end users cannot do, and the financial effect

Customer communications

What customers have been told, through which channel, and when the next update is due

Recovery

Current estimate, confidence level, next update time

Leadership action

The decision or intervention needed, or “none at this time”

Results

  • Al Rajhi Bank. The standup and morning report replaced ad-hoc requests by email, phone and messaging, and cut ad-hoc communication during major outages by 80%. The standup also gave the CTO a direct forum to clear cross-department resource blockers.
  • Crisis escalation. I introduced a Major Incident and Crisis Management Interlocking Protocol for P1 incidents that qualify as crises. It separates incident response from crisis escalation and defines who owns which decisions.
  • Marqeta, regional cloud outage. I activated the crisis management protocol to align engineering and executive leadership. The 30-minute synchronized reports reduced executive inquiry volume. I then formalized the gaps into a Crisis-Incident Handoff Playbook.
  • Governance reporting. Major Incident Reports and dashboards with failure-mode analysis for weekly reviews at Al Rajhi Bank, and the Major Incident Executive Report structure and workflow at Marqeta.
