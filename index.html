Major incident command

At Al Rajhi Bank, I led high-priority incident bridges and commanded my shifts during a full data centre switchover exercise. At Marqeta, I used the same command structure to coordinate engineering and communications during a regional cloud outage.

I led P1/P2 bridges across core banking, payments infrastructure and managed service environments, using observability and monitoring tools for real-time status and impact analysis. My tooling experience spans Datadog, Dynatrace, Grafana, SolarWinds and TrueSight.

The first 30 minutes

In the first 30 minutes, I establish which services and customers are affected, confirm who leads technical recovery and who owns communications, and agree on the next mitigation step. I set the next update time immediately. Root cause analysis continues after service is stable.

Incidents, exercises and operational changes

  • Peak-load operations. I designed and led 12 monthly payroll hypercare bridges, each running 48 hours. They covered the bank’s most critical recurring processing window and brought infrastructure, core banking, online banking and mainframe teams together with senior directors.
  • Disaster recovery. I served as Major Incident Manager on three disaster recovery (DR) drills. On each shift, I coordinated engineers and directors on the bridge, briefed the DR committee and handed command to the incoming lead. The largest was an 11-day drill that simulated a cybersecurity incident and required a full switchover to the alternate data centre. During the exercise, the bank processed more than 1 billion transactions without interrupting customer service.
  • Change control during incidents. I evaluated and implemented emergency changes and led 42 checks with Command Center agents to verify normal service after scheduled changes. I introduced a handoff for post-implementation verification between the incident and change management teams.
  • Command structure. I introduced a business-impact severity matrix based on the impact and urgency model, and co-developed the accountability boundaries between Unified Command Center teams with the Process Improvement Director.
  • Observability. I partnered with the SRE and observability team on 134 service journey maps in Dynatrace, to trace critical services across their dependencies.

Incident decisions at Marqeta and NCR

  • AWS us-east-1 regional outage (Marqeta). The outage affected at least 11 critical services. I consolidated the related tickets under one parent incident and separated the technical commander and communications lead roles.
  • Weekend outage of 35 school sites (NCR). As the only shift lead on duty, with limited engineering and dispatch capacity, I opened a war room, escalated the resourcing limit to the service management office, agreed a dispatch plan with the network provider and shared a status list at the end of every shift.

At Interac

Critical processing windows, resilience exercises and participant-facing incidents call for the same discipline: one commander, clear roles, customer impact mitigated first, and communication on a fixed cadence from the first minutes.

Executive and customer communication

I use one incident record to brief two audiences. Customers need to know which services they can use and when they will hear from us again. Executives also need to know which decisions require their intervention.

What goes into an update

Each update states the customer impact, the customer communications plan, the recovery estimate and whether leadership needs to act. When the recovery estimate is still uncertain, I say so and keep the promised update time.

Update format

Customer impact
Which customers are affected and their tiers, what those customers and their end users cannot do, and the financial effect
Customer communications
What customers have been told, through which channel, and when the next update is due
Recovery
Current estimate, confidence level, next update time
Leadership action
The decision or intervention needed, or “none at this time”
P1 Major Incident Update 3, 10:45 ET Illustrative incident scenario
Customer impact
Since 09:52 ET, cardholders at 14 customers have experienced declined or delayed payments. For Tier 1 customers, failures are concentrated in store. We have identified no data loss.
3 Tier 16 Tier 25 Tier 3
Customer communications
Status page updated at 10:05 and 10:40. Tier 1 customers have been notified directly by their account managers. Next customer update due by 11:20.
Recovery
Engineering has isolated the issue to a database failover and is moving traffic to the secondary cluster. Estimated recovery is 11:30 ET, with medium confidence. The next internal update is at 11:15 ET.
Leadership action
Please approve the wording of the next Tier 1 customer notification by 11:00 ET.

What I measure

  • Time to first customer communication. Captured on every incident record by an agent that reads the incident and customer channels and writes the first notification time into Jira.
  • Communication within SLA. Tracked per Triage Coordinator in a monthly scorecard of 15 KPIs, reconciled across Jira, PagerDuty and Slack.

32% reduction in mean time to communicate with customers, quarter over quarter (Marqeta). I co-designed and deployed the Inci-Bot comms agent. It produces a first draft in about two minutes. A production engineer or Major Incident Manager reviews it before publication.

Results

  • Al Rajhi Bank. The standup and morning report replaced ad hoc requests by email, phone and messaging, and cut ad hoc communication during major outages by 80%. The standup gave the CTO a direct forum to clear cross-department resource blockers.
  • Crisis escalation. I introduced a Major Incident and Crisis Management Interlocking Protocol for P1 incidents that qualify as crises. It separates incident response from crisis escalation and defines who owns which decisions.
  • Marqeta, regional cloud outage. I activated the crisis management protocol to align engineering and executive leadership. The 30-minute synchronized reports reduced executive inquiry volume. I then formalized the gaps into a Crisis-Incident Handoff Playbook.

At Interac

Running a customer communications bridge in parallel with the technical bridge works best when the format, cadence and approval path are agreed in advance and time to communicate is measured on every incident.

Building a problem management program

After service is restored, I work with engineering to identify recurring failures, assign corrective actions and track them to closure. At Marqeta, that meant building a formal problem management process and reviewing the quality of post-incident reports.

Building Marqeta’s problem management program

When I joined, Marqeta had no formal problem management process. I built it in phases, proving each part before scaling it.

  1. Foundation

    I defined the workflow, completion criteria and known-error template. I set up a Confluence space and Jira project, linked incidents to problem records with automation rules, and backfilled records for five frequent recurring problems.

    Phase 1, complete

  2. Pilot

    I selected a pilot team and problem owner, gathered engineering feedback and agreed on reporting intervals. For the first problem record, I mapped work already underway, coordinated a failover test and began a 12-week observation period against baseline incident frequency and impact. I also introduced a process to reconcile change records with problem records.

    Phase 2, in progress

  3. Scale

    Before recommending a wider rollout, I would review recurrence and customer impact with the pilot owner and agree on any workflow changes.

Improving incident performance

  • Risk, Fraud and Dispute engineering (Marqeta). I assessed how the teams handled incidents, built an action item focus board and backlog health dashboard, and reported quarterly results. For those teams, mean time to resolution fell 39% and the backlog of post-incident actions fell 64%.
  • Post-incident quality (Marqeta). I created a rubric for post-incident reviews and a monthly Post-Mortem Bar Raiser review across engineering, focused on recurring root causes and action item completion.
  • Root cause support (Al Rajhi Bank). I worked with problem management on deep-dive root cause analyses, providing trend analysis and corrective action plans that reduced recurring incident volume.

Documentation that holds up during an incident

  • Al Rajhi Bank. As the Command Center’s knowledge subject matter expert, I worked with the Knowledge Management team to migrate 950+ articles into the new Command Center space in Confluence. I also updated the standard operating procedures for Major Incident Managers.
  • Marqeta. I wrote the Incident Management Process and Procedures document, process flowchart, responsibility matrix and escalation policy, and built the problem management knowledge base with a known error database.
  • NCR. I modernized the incident management playbooks and workflow diagrams.

At Interac

Continuous improvement and an incident documentation framework both depend on the same thing: a clear owner for every recurring cause and every action item, with records people can find during an incident.

Automation and AI agents

My automation work started with a dispatch decision at NCR: did a site still need an engineer? At Marqeta, I applied the same question to reporting: which steps can software handle, and which decisions need a person?

  1. Visibility: NCR

    Field engineers were being sent to sites where service had already recovered, because restoration notices were buried in ticket logs. I built a ServiceNow dashboard matching active dispatches against restoration signals, added an automation that scanned tickets for those signals, and connected it to Slack so case owners were alerted to cancel the dispatch.

    Unnecessary field dispatches fell 65% within 90 days.

  2. Integration: Al Rajhi Bank

    I integrated real-time P1 status SMS with ServiceNow, so the Executive Team had live updates while ServiceNow remained the single source of truth.

  3. Workflow automation: Marqeta

    At Marqeta, Slack workflows support customer incident reports, status page updates and major incident reports. They also handle maintenance notifications, triage handovers and executive alerts. A PagerDuty workflow with Jira Automation adds the incident’s Slack channel to its Jira ticket.

    These workflows automated 40% of the incident team’s manual tasks.

  4. Agents: Marqeta

    Inci-Bot reads Slack transcripts, Jira records and war room notes and produces a structured first draft of post-incident reviews and customer-facing incident reports. I co-designed and deployed Inci-Bot, then refined its report drafts with Legal and Technical Writing. Documentation turnaround fell from 5 to 2 business days.

    Inci-Bot comms agent produces a first customer update in about two minutes. A production engineer or Major Incident Manager reviews it before publication. Mean time to first customer communication fell 32% quarter over quarter.

    Notification agent. A Rovo agent with Jira Automation records when partners first received an incident notification. The agent distinguishes the original notification from a later confirmation.

    Reusable AI skills for known error records, time-to-communicate analysis and the Triage Coordinator scorecard.

Human review

Inci-Bot launched as a beta. When some summaries included details unsupported by the incident record, I revised the prompts and required an Incident Manager to sign off before wider use. Every customer-facing draft is reviewed by a person before it is published.

At Interac

In a regulated payments environment, automation should first remove the manual steps that slow incident response and communication, with clear human accountability for every customer-facing output.

Cross-functional leadership

Most of the people on an incident bridge report to someone else. Before an incident, I work with their leads to agree who can make a decision, who needs to be consulted and how the next shift takes over.

How I build alignment

  1. Map the stakeholders

    Identify the teams affected, the decisions they own and how their daily work would change.

  2. Meet the teams individually

    Ask each team where the current process slows them down and what they need from the next team.

  3. Identify handoff problems

    Trace a recent handoff and identify where ownership or information was lost.

  4. Draft, then refine with each team

    Every affected team shapes the framework before it is final.

  5. Agree on a sponsor

    Confirm which leader owns the outcome and can resolve cross-team dependencies.

  6. Put the agreement into practice

    Train the people who run it, set an update cadence and review how it performs.

Examples of cross-team leadership

  • NCR runbook alignment. NOC, engineering and escalation teams used different runbooks. Following these steps removed ownership ambiguity during P1 incidents and made escalation faster and more decisive.
  • NCR after-hours change approval. Major Incident Managers were asked to act as change manager outside business hours. I worked individually with the NOC supervisor, the Quality and Process team and management, and agreed training, engagement conditions for the on-call change manager, and priority for the team in future change management roles.
  • Al Rajhi Bank payroll hypercare and DR. 12 bridges brought senior directors and four technical teams into one view of status and ownership. On DR drills I handed over the bridge at each shift and reported to the DR committee.
  • Marqeta training as an OKR. Presenting incident training as a Q4 organizational OKR secured leadership backing and protected time: 384 engineers trained, with 79% completion in one quarter. The training became part of onboarding.

Developing people

  • NCR. I delivered a three-day ServiceNow and ITIL course for new hires and recorded it for onboarding. Two participants moved into junior ITSM analyst roles.
  • NCR. I covered for my manager for three months, held one-on-ones and used an anonymous retrospective board to help the team agree on improvements.
  • Al Rajhi Bank. Recorded training for Command Center agents on incident practices and communication with Major Incident Managers.
  • Marqeta. Trained and onboarded the 24/7 incident triage team and published its handover process.

At Interac

I would spend the first weeks in one-on-one conversations with the technology, risk, communications, operations and problem management leads before proposing any change to how incidents are run.

First 90 days at Interac

This is my proposed starting plan. I would first learn how Interac’s teams handle incidents and participant communications, then agree with the team on one improvement worth piloting.

Crawl

Days 1–30 · Understand the operating model

  • Shadow responders during live incidents and trace a recent escalation from detection to the first participant update.
  • Meet individually with technology, risk, communications, operations and problem management leads.
  • Review recent major incident reports, open action items, severity definitions and on-call coverage.
  • Deliverable: a map of escalation paths and decision owners, validated with responders.

Walk

Days 31–60 · Test one improvement

  • Agree on one improvement with the incident management lead, such as participant update timing or ownership of overdue actions.
  • Pilot it with one team and measure it against a baseline.
  • Deliverable: pilot results and a recommendation to expand, adjust or stop the change.

Run

Days 61–90 · Decide what to expand

  • Scale what worked, with named owners and training for the incident management team.
  • Review the pilot measures and responder feedback with the incident management lead.
  • Deliverable: a comparison with the baseline and a proposed roadmap for the next two quarters.

Measures I would establish with the team

  • Mean time to resolution by severity, using agreed start and end timestamps
  • Time to first customer communication
  • Percentage of post-incident actions closed by their agreed due date
  • Rate of incidents linked to a previously identified root cause
