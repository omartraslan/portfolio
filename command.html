Major incident command

The first 30 minutesResponse examplesOther incidents

Senior Major Incident & Problem Manager at the Al Rajhi Bank IT Command Center, delivered through Cisco managed services, July 2024 to July 2025. Al Rajhi Bank is the world’s largest Islamic bank and ranks first in Saudi Arabia by customers and total financing. The IT Command Center ran 24/7/365.

Scope

I led P1/P2 bridges across core banking, payments infrastructure and managed service environments. I used observability and monitoring tools to get real-time incident status and drive impact analysis during live bridges. My tooling experience spans Datadog, Dynatrace, Grafana, SolarWinds and TrueSight.

The first 30 minutes

The first half hour of a major incident sets its direction. In that window I establish a clear shared understanding of the situation, define roles and accountability, and focus the team on mitigating customer impact before pursuing root cause. Communication starts at once, to internal and external stakeholders, on a fixed cadence, so everyone works from the same information while we manage against SLAs.

Evidence

  • Peak-load operations. I designed and led 12 monthly payroll hypercare bridges, each running 48 hours. They covered the bank’s most critical recurring processing window and brought infrastructure, core banking, online banking and mainframe teams together with senior directors.
  • Disaster recovery. I took part in 3 disaster recovery drills as a Major Incident Manager. In each one I ran the DR bridge on my shift and handed it over at every shift change. I worked with the engineering teams and directors on the bridge and reported to the DR committee at the end of each shift. The largest was an 11-day drill that simulated a cybersecurity incident and required a full switchover to the alternate data center. It validated continuous delivery of critical banking services, staff operations from alternate work locations and communication across all channels. It processed more than 1 billion transactions with no interruption to customer service.
  • Change control during incidents. I evaluated and implemented emergency changes and led 42 post-change window business-as-usual verifications, run with the Command Center agents’ weekly post-change jobs according to the change management schedule. I also introduced a handoff for post-implementation verification between the incident and change management teams.
  • Command structure. I introduced a business-impact severity matrix based on the impact and urgency model. I also co-developed the accountability boundaries between Unified Command Center teams with the Process Improvement Director.
  • Observability. I partnered with the SRE and observability team on 134 service journey mappings in Dynatrace, giving structured visibility across the bank’s critical services.

Also commanded

  • AWS us-east-1 regional outage (Marqeta). 11+ critical services were affected. I consolidated the related tickets under one parent incident and separated the technical commander and communications lead roles.
  • NCR Global Command Center. I led P1/P2 bridges for global banking and enterprise clients with resolver teams of 8 to 12 engineers. One example is a weekend outage that took down 35 school sites, handled with limited on-shift resources.
